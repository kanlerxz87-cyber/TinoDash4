Decision log

Fifteen decisions to record with a date, an owner and a reason, so a future reader knows what was chosen and why.

Decide, write it down, and say why

The household asked that the decision and the date be recorded next to each item, so a future reader knows what was chosen and why. This is the log. It is intentionally mostly empty: filling it in is the household's job, and an entry written before the fact is worth more than a reconstruction afterwards.

Rules for this log

  1. Append, never edit. If a decision changes, add a new row with a new date and reference the row it supersedes. A log that can be rewritten is not a log.
  2. Record the reason, not just the choice. “Sell” is not a decision; “sell because we do not intend to live in Property A and the appraised value exceeded our stated trigger” is.
  3. Record who decided. A decision with no owner drifts.
  4. Record what was assumed. The model version and the input set, so a later reader can tell whether the decision was made on the same facts they are looking at.

The entries a future reader will most want, and that are most often missing

“We chose to rent it out instead”

If that entry ever appears, the reader needs to know it was made with knowledge that it forfeits the Prop 19 exclusion, and what the reassessed tax bill turned out to be. Without that, the next generation will repeat it.

“We changed our mind in year 12”

Changing a plan during a drawdown is the single most common way these plans fail. The log records whether the change was made on new facts or on new feelings, which is the only way anyone can learn from it.

“We never got the appraisal”

This is the entry that most needs to exist. If the decision was made on an unverified household estimate, say so plainly, date it, and put the flag next to it. An honest record of an unverified decision is worth more than a clean-looking one.

This is a decision-support model, not legal, tax or investment advice. Every figure traces to a verified authority or an explicit assumption. Unverified items are on Flagged for review. Confirm everything with a California estate attorney, a CPA, and a fee-only fiduciary adviser before acting.
