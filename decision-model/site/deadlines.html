Deadlines as a timeline

Thirteen deadlines, ordered by when they bite, with the statutory basis for each and an honest note on which ones are actually irreversible.

The deadlines, as a timeline

Ordered by when they bite, not by when they are usually discussed. The two that actually destroy money are the one-year occupancy deadline and the nine-month Form 706 date; everything else can be fixed late, at a price.

Before the death

Confirm the trust holds the house; ask about portability and prior taxable gifts; get the capital-improvement records; decide trust-continues vs outright distribution.

Basis: none — this is the only phase with no clock  ·  Who: attorney, CPA

Day 0

Date of death. Fair market value is fixed on this date, and it becomes the income-tax basis, the Prop 19 test value, and the Form 706 value simultaneously.

Basis: the value is fixed; it cannot be re-chosen later  ·  Who: appraiser

Days 1–30

Order death certificates; secure the property and notify the insurer; confirm the successor trustee; locate the deed and tax bills.

Basis: no statutory deadline, but the vacancy endorsement and the security of the property are time-sensitive  ·  Who: trustee

Within 1 year

Occupy the home as a principal residence, and file the homeowners' exemption (BOE-266).

Basis: RTC § 63.2(a)(1)(A)–(B) — hard, no extension  ·  Who: heir

February 15 following

Homeowners' exemption filing deadline for the full year's benefit.

Basis: RTC § 255.5  ·  Who: heir

9 months

Form 706 due, extendable to 15 months. Required if the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the exclusion, and needed to elect portability even when no tax is due.

Basis: IRC § 6075(a); Reg. § 20.2010-2(a)(1)  ·  Who: CPA

Within 3 years

File the Prop 19 exclusion claim on BOE-19-P — but not earlier than the occupancy and exemption conditions can be met, and not later than a transfer to a third party.

Basis: RTC § 63.2(f)(1)(A)  ·  Who: heir

5 years

Last chance for the simplified late portability election, and only for estates not otherwise required to file.

Basis: Rev. Proc. 2022-32 § 4.01(1)  ·  Who: CPA

January 2 – March 31, annually

Informal decline-in-value review window for single-family residential property.

Basis: SF Assessor procedure  ·  Who: owner

July 2 – September 15, annually

Assessment appeal filing window with the Assessment Appeals Board.

Basis: RTC § 1603  ·  Who: owner

April 15 / June 15 / September 15 / January 15

California estimated tax payment dates. A large gain needs a safe-harbour calculation before the first one after the sale.

Basis: Form 540-ES  ·  Who: CPA

Every year, forever

Property tax bills (two instalments), insurance renewal, and the annual model re-run.

Basis: —  ·  Who: owner

Every 2 years, odd February

The BOE reprices the Prop 19 allowance. The next repricing is February 2027 and is not yet published.

Basis: Cal. Const. art. XIII A § 2.1(c)(4)  ·  Who: —

The one piece of good news in the deadline list

Almost every other deadline is forgiving. The Prop 19 claim has three years. The portability election has five. Assessment appeals run every year. The one thing that cannot be fixed later is occupancy — and unlike the others, it is entirely within the household's control. That is a genuinely favourable asymmetry, and it means the decision is less deadline-driven than it looks.
