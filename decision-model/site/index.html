Executive summary

A read-first summary of a 30-year, after-tax comparison of nine ways to deal with an inherited house in Santa Clara County, held alongside a three-unit rental building already owned in San Francisco.

Decision support · updated 2026-11-25 · all figures rounded to the nearest 10 dollar

On the numbers, selling and diversifying wins by $23.18M over 30 years — but the answer is not robust, and three specific things would overturn it.

And the number that matters most is not in this model at all: whether the household actually wants to live in Santa Clara County for decades, because the entire tax advantage of holding the house is legally conditioned on living in it.

What would change the answer

Written down before the figures, so the outcome cannot be reverse-engineered from one projection. If any of these hold, the ranking moves.

1

Living there for a long time, and meaning it

The hold path is only cheap because Proposition 19 keeps the assessed value near its current base. Under RTC § 63.2(a)(1)(B)–(C) that benefit is removed the moment the heir stops being eligible for the homeowners' exemption.

Computed, not asserted: claiming Proposition 19 is worth $561,920 to the hold path over 30 years — real money, but only about 2.4% of the gap to selling. If occupancy is a “maybe”, the hold path is not really available at all.

RTC § 63.2 — official California code text

2

The value estimate is wrong

The $3,100,000 figure is a household estimate, not an appraisal. A ±10% error moves the sell path by more than most of the policy variables in this model combined, because the sale price is also the tax basis, the Prop 19 arithmetic and the portfolio principal.

A 10% lower appraised value costs the sell path roughly $3.07M and the hold path far less.

Flagged: household estimate, unverified

3

South Bay house prices outrun equities for decades

The model assumes equities return more than housing over the horizon. That is a forecast, not a fact. Across the five regimes the gap between selling and holding ranges from −$8,900 to $56.93M, and in one regime holding wins.

This is a forecast disagreement, not a fact. Nobody knows.

See the stress grid on Black swans

A fourth candidate — “the inherited cash is large enough that the estate already exceeds the federal exclusion” — does not change the ranking. It changes the timing of the decision, because the largest single tax exposure in this file is a federal estate tax on an estate that may already be near $15,000,000, and selling the house does not reduce it. The finding the household should hear first

“Rent it out and keep the low property tax base” does not exist under current California law. Renting requires not occupying; not occupying within one year forfeits the Proposition 19 exclusion; and the property is reassessed to full market value. The model runs both cases and reports the difference, rather than asserting a story: at the 2045 transfer the assessed value goes from $5.23M with the exclusion to $6.29M without it, and the property tax collected over the 30 years rises by $561,920. Every plan that quietly assumes both rental income and a Prop 13 basis is built on a rule that no longer exists.

What this model is not

It is not a recommendation. The household asked for a model that maximises profit and lowers cost; on those terms the answer is a sale. But the model cannot price the thing that most likely dominates the real decision: whether the household wants to live in a particular house in a particular town. That is a preference, not a variable. If it turns out the household wants to live there, the correct comparison is not sell-versus-hold, it is live-then-sell versus live-forever, and the difference between those two is only $7.05M.

Three things to do before anything else

  1. Get the value right. An appraisal or at minimum a broker CMA for both properties. Every path is denominated in that number and it is currently a guess. Detail
  2. Answer the occupancy question in writing. Not “probably”. The Prop 19 benefit is worth $561,920 of terminal net worth over 30 years by itself — and it is forfeited by moving out, which is why the answer has to be a commitment rather than an intention. Pre-commitment agreement
  3. Confirm whether the estate is near the federal exclusion. At $15,000,000 per person in 2026 the answer is probably no, but “a considerable amount of money” plus two Bay Area houses plus 25 years of growth can get there, and the fix is a planning conversation that must happen before the death, not after.
