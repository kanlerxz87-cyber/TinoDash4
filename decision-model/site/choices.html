The discipline: decide the criteria before the numbers arrive

Reverse-engineering is the failure mode of every projection exercise. If you look at the answer first, you will find reasons to like it. So the criteria are fixed here, in advance, with explicit trigger values. Whoever reads this in 2045 should be able to tell whether the household chose consistently with what it said it would choose.

What the model would have to believe for holding to win

A useful robustness check: instead of asking which path scores highest, ask what would have to be true for the other path to win. If the required belief is absurd, the ranking is safe. If it is merely optimistic, the ranking is not safe.

For live-and-hold to beat sell-and-invest

  • Property A house prices must grow at least as fast as a diversified equity portfolio in nominal terms, for 30 years, in a town whose value is tied to one industry.
  • The household must occupy continuously for the whole period. One move destroys the Prop 19 base and with it the entire advantage.
  • Maintenance, insurance and utilities must stay near the low end of the range. In the high case they consume the whole holding advantage.
  • No Proposition 8 decline-in-value event long enough to reset the assessment downward — that one actually helps the hold path, and it is why the hold path is relatively better in the crash scenarios.

For sell-and-invest to beat live-and-hold

  • Equities must beat Bay Area housing over the horizon. Historically true nationally; not obviously true for Property A over specific 30-year windows.
  • The household must be able to hold the portfolio through a 50%+ drawdown without selling. Empirically this is the assumption that fails most often.
  • The after-tax proceeds must actually be reinvested rather than spent. The model cannot enforce this; only the investment policy statement can.
  • The household must be indifferent to living in this particular house.

The asymmetry nobody mentions

The hold path is partially hedged against a property crash by Proposition 8: if market value falls below the factored base year value, the assessment can be reduced on request, which cuts the property tax. The sell path has no equivalent protection against an equity crash. This is a real, structural advantage of holding that appears in the 1929 and 2008 scenarios and is routinely omitted from comparisons like this one.
