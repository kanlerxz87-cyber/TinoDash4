Does San Francisco Proposition H apply, at what rate, and can it be passed through?

LOW — the dollar amount is immaterial, but the rent pass-through interacts with the tenancies and should be understood

What we have: Partly known. Prop H passed on 3 November 2026 with about 72% of the vote, imposing an annual parcel tax from 2027 for 15 years. A three-unit building is a multifamily residential parcel: $249 for the first 5,000 square feet plus $0.195 per square foot above that, capped at $50,000. Suggestions for discovering the missing information

The household asked for suggestions on how to find what is missing, not just a list of what is missing. These are ordered by cost-effectiveness.

  1. One afternoon with the county assessor's website. The tax rate area, the assessed value, the exemption history and the parcel page are all public. This answers three HIGH-priority questions at zero cost and closes the largest uncertainty in the property-tax model.
  2. Two phone calls to insurance brokers. Real quotes, not estimates. Ask about fire severity zone and FAIR Plan eligibility at the same time. This converts the single largest placeholder in the operating-cost model into a fact.
  3. One appraisal. Roughly $600–$900 for a retrospective date-of-death appraisal. It is simultaneously the basis for income tax, the value test for Prop 19, and the estate-tax valuation. It is the highest-return dollar in this entire file.
  4. One hour with a property manager. A rent survey for 95014 single-family homes turns the rent path from a rule-of-thumb band into evidence. Ask what rents, not what is listed.
  5. One meeting with the CPA, with a specific agenda. Prior taxable gifts, portability, and the projected estate against the exclusion. The briefing sheet page is built to be the agenda for this meeting.
  6. One meeting with the attorney, with two specific questions. (a) When does the Prop 19 occupancy clock start if a trust holds the house? (b) What does the trust say about distribution? Everything else can wait.
  7. One five-year capital plan. A contractor walkthrough of the roof, sewer, HVAC, electrical and foundation. This is the number that most often turns a hold decision into a sell decision, and it is invisible in a percentage-of-value model.
