Glossary

The vocabulary this decision is made in. Every term is defined the way it is used in this file, which is not always the way it is used loosely elsewhere — several of the most expensive mistakes in inherited-property decisions come from a word being understood differently by the heir, the attorney and the assessor.

TermDefinition as used here
Adjusted basisWhat you have invested in a property for tax purposes: original cost plus improvements, minus depreciation taken. Used to compute gain on sale. See § 1014 for the inherited case.
Assessment appeals boardThe county body that hears disputes about assessed value. In San Francisco, appeals are filed July 2 – September 15.
Behavioral Health Services TaxCalifornia's 1% tax on taxable income above $1,000,000. Formerly the Mental Health Services Tax. Computed on Form 540 line 62. The threshold is not indexed.
Basis step-upThe reset of an asset's basis to fair market value at the owner's death, under IRC § 1014. Eliminates the income tax on gain accrued during the decedent's life.
BOE-19-PThe claim form for the Proposition 19 parent-child or grandparent-grandchild exclusion.
BOE-266The claim form for California's homeowners' property tax exemption ($7,000 of assessed value).
Costa-HawkinsThe California statute (Civ. Code § 1954.53) that allows a rent-controlled unit's rent to reset to market when a tenancy ends. Vacancy decontrol.
Decline-in-value (Prop 8)A reduction in assessed value to market value when the market has fallen below the factored base year value. Requested from the assessor; not automatic.
Deed-in-lieuA transfer of title to a lender instead of foreclosure. Rare in this file but appears in transfer-tax exemption lists.
Depreciation (rental)An annual tax deduction for wear on a rental building. Residential rental property uses straight line over 27.5 years, mid-month convention, on the building only — never the land.
DSUEDeceased spousal unused exclusion. The portion of a deceased spouse's estate tax exclusion that a surviving spouse can use, if portability is elected.
Factored base year valueA property's 1975-76 (or acquisition-year) assessed value, increased each year by at most 2% under Proposition 13. This is the number the property tax is actually computed on.
Fair market value (FMV)The price a willing buyer would pay a willing seller. At death, this is the number that sets basis, the Prop 19 test, and the estate tax value — all three, simultaneously.
FAIR PlanCalifornia's insurer of last resort. Relevant when the admitted market will not write a policy, which is increasingly common in fire-exposed areas.
Form 706The federal estate tax return. Due nine months after death, extendable to fifteen. Also the vehicle for electing portability.
Gift tax annual exclusionThe amount one person can give another each year without it counting against the lifetime exemption. $19,000 per recipient in 2026.
Gross rent multiplier (GRM)Sale price divided by annual gross rent. The standard valuation metric for small multifamily buildings, and why a single-family per-square-foot estimate misleads for Property B.
Imputed rentThe rental value a homeowner consumes by living in their own property. Economically real, tax-free, and easily forgotten — omitting it makes selling look better than it is.
Net investment income tax (NIIT)A 3.8% federal surtax on investment income above $250,000 MAGI for joint filers. Gain excluded under § 121 is not subject to it.
PortabilityThe election that lets a surviving spouse use a deceased spouse's unused estate tax exclusion. Requires a timely Form 706.
Proposition 8A 1978 measure allowing a temporary reduction in assessed value when market value falls below the Prop 13 base. The hold path's built-in hedge against a crash.
Proposition 13The 1978 constitutional amendment capping the property tax rate at 1% and annual assessment growth at 2%, with reassessment on change of ownership or new construction.
Proposition 19The 2020 ballot measure, effective 16 February 2021 for parent-child transfers, that narrowed the inherited-home exclusion: only a principal residence qualifies, the heir must occupy within one year, and the excluded amount is capped at the factored base year value plus an inflation-adjusted $1,000,000.
Proposition H (2026)San Francisco's November 2026 parcel tax funding Muni operations for 15 years, from 2027. $129 for a single-family parcel, $249 for multifamily, $799 for non-residential, plus marginal amounts on large buildings.
Recapture (depreciation)The tax on depreciation previously deducted, charged when the property is sold. Unrecaptured § 1250 gain is capped at 25% federally; California taxes it as ordinary income.
Section 121The federal exclusion of up to $250,000 (single) or $500,000 (joint) of gain on the sale of a principal residence, requiring ownership and use for 2 of the last 5 years.
Section 1031Like-kind exchange. Defers gain when investment real property is exchanged for other investment real property. Requires identification within 45 days and completion within 180.
Section 1014The basis rule for property acquired from a decedent: basis equals fair market value at the date of death.
Tax rate area (TRA)A code identifying the combination of taxing agencies that apply to a parcel. Determines the actual property tax rate, which is why two neighbouring houses can have different effective rates.
Tenancy-in-common (TIC)A form of co-ownership of a single parcel. Relevant in San Francisco but not, on the household's description, to either property here.
Vacancy decontrolSee Costa-Hawkins. The ability to reset rent to market when a rent-controlled tenancy ends.

The five words most often confused

Market valueWhat it would sell for. Sets the § 1014 basis, the Prop 19 value test, and the estate tax value — all from the same appraisal, on the same date.
Assessed valueWhat the property tax is computed on. Under Prop 13 this is the factored base year value, which can be a small fraction of market value and is normally nowhere near it.
BasisWhat the income tax is computed on when you sell. At death it resets to market value. Unrelated to assessed value, even though both are sometimes called “the basis”.
ExclusionA statutory rule that removes something from tax. Prop 19's is an exclusion from reassessment; § 121's is an exclusion from income. They are different taxes and different mechanics.
ExemptionA reduction in the amount taxed. The homeowners' exemption removes $7,000 from assessed value — but its real importance here is that filing it is a legal condition of the Prop 19 exclusion.
