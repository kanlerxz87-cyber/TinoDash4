Method, sources & privacy

What was verified, what was not, how to check it, and the privacy model that governs what may appear here.

How this was built, and what its limits are

The household asked for line-by-line verification from official sources, no hallucination, and links for manual review. This page explains what was actually done, what was not, and how to check any of it.

The verification protocol

  1. Every figure that enters the model is a row in data/facts.json. The row records the value, the issuing authority, the instrument, the URL, the exact wording read, the date checked, and a status.
  2. A status of verified means I opened the cited page or PDF in this session and read the value in its text. The quote field holds the actual wording. Nothing is marked verified on the basis of memory or a summary.
  3. A status of secondary means the value is official in substance but I could not fetch the official page. These are listed on the Flagged page with the reason.
  4. A status of unverified means no official source exists or could be located. The value is either withheld, or shown as a range, or shown as the household's own estimate clearly labelled. It is never presented as a fact.
  5. Nothing was inferred to fill a gap. Where a number was needed and could not be established, the model carries a range and the site says so.

What was actually fetched and read

Among others: the IRS estate-tax page and Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (both the PDF and the sections cited); IRS Publications 551, Topics 409, 414, 415, 701, 703; the IRS net investment income tax page; Revenue Procedure 2022-32 in full; the complete text of IRC §§ 102, 1014, 1031 and 168 via Cornell's Legal Information Institute; the California Franchise Tax Board's 2025 Form 540 booklet (including the line 62 Behavioral Health Services Tax instructions, which the household specifically asked about); Revenue and Taxation Code § 63.2 in full including the 2025 amendment; State Board of Equalization Letter To Assessors 2025/009; the Board of Equalization homeowners' exemption page; the San Francisco Assessor-Recorder's Proposition 19 and transfer-tax pages; the Federal Reserve H.15 release; FRED's CPI series; and the NBER's business cycle table.

What was not done

  • No legislation tracking. The household asked for proposed legislation to be tracked and distinguished from enacted law. This site tracks neither. It records only enacted law as of 2026-11-25 and says so on every page that touches the question.
  • No appraisal, no comparables, no rent survey. The three numbers that most drive the result are household estimates. This is the model's largest weakness and it is flagged in the most prominent place available.
  • No professional review. No attorney, CPA or adviser has seen this.
  • No probabilistic modelling. The scenarios are not weighted. Assigning probabilities to 1929 versus 2022 would be inventing information.
  • Property B is not fully modelled. It is analysed as a risk factor and a liquidity constraint, not as a cash-flow asset with its own ledger. That is a real gap.

Privacy

This repository is public. Plan accordingly.

The repository is public and its GitHub Pages site is public and cannot be made private on a free plan. The household's stated highest priority was that no personal information appear in the final product. The measures taken:

  • No names, addresses, parcel numbers, account numbers or dates of death appear anywhere in this repository or on this site. None were collected.
  • All dollar figures on this site are rounded to the nearest 10 dollar, as requested.
  • Parcel-unique assessed values are banded by construction, not by rounding. A $10 band on a tax-assessed value is still unique to one parcel; so those two values are carried as $10,000 bands in the model and never rendered at full precision.
  • No analytics, no cookies, no third-party scripts, no fonts loaded from a CDN, and no forms that post anywhere. A visitor cannot be profiled and their inputs are never transmitted.
  • The .gitignore blocks private/, *.private.json and inventory.local.* so that exact figures cannot be committed by accident.

The honest limitation: rounding protects against casual discovery and search indexing. It does not protect against a reader who already knows the household. If that is a realistic threat, do not publish — run python3 sitegen.py --out site-private locally and keep the output off the internet. See PRIVACY.md in the repository.

Git history

The repository was created with a single initial commit containing an 11-byte README. No personal data has ever been committed, so there is no pre-rounding history to scrub — the first version of every data file is already the rounded, banded version. The rule going forward is that a private figure must never be committed even transiently, because git history is permanent and a git rm commit does not remove it from history.

Reproducing and extending this

python3 sitegen.py --out siteBuild the site. No dependencies beyond the Python standard library.
python3 sitegen.py --round 10000Coarser rounding for a more privacy-hardened build.
python3 tools/check_site.py siteCheck every internal link, every image, and every claim that a figure has a source.
data/facts.jsonThe single source of truth for every figure. Edit here, never in a generated page.
model/params.pyEvery input, with its provenance and status.
model/engine.pyThe simulation. Heavily commented on the legal reasoning, because the legal reasoning is where these models usually go wrong.
model/runner.pyThe tables the site publishes.
COORDINATION.mdThe task board, for parallel sessions.
PRIVACY.mdThe privacy model, binding on every file.
