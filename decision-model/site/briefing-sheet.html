2. The four questions we need answered

For the attorneyIf a trust holds the house and the beneficiary occupies it, when does the one-year occupancy clock in RTC § 63.2(a)(1)(A) start — the date of death, or the date of distribution? And does the trust need to distribute the property outright for the beneficiary to be the “transferee” under § 63.2(e)(9)?
For the CPAIs there any prior taxable gift or unclaimed portability in this family? Concretely: has a prior spouse died, and was a Form 706 filed? Is the five-year window under Rev. Proc. 2022-32 still open?
For whoever projects the estateWhat is the projected gross estate at the expected date of death, against a federal exclusion of $15,000,000 per person in 2026, indexed?
For the assessorWhat is the tax rate area for Property A and the published total rate for the current fiscal year?

4. The three legal points that drive the numbers

  1. Prop 19 requires continuing occupancy. RTC § 63.2(a)(1)(B)–(C): the exclusion is removed when the heir stops being eligible for the homeowners' exemption, and a new base year value is enrolled. “Rent it out and keep the low tax base” is not available for transfers after 16 February 2021.
  2. The $1,000,000 allowance is inflation-adjusted and unpublished for 2045. Currently $1,044,590 for transfers 2025-02-16 to 2027-02-15 (BOE LTA 2025/009). Modelled as three growth scenarios rather than a point estimate.
  3. The § 1014 basis resets to date-of-death value. Selling promptly therefore realises almost no gain; the cost of selling is the ~6% transaction cost, not income tax. Selling later manufactures a taxable gain taxed at California ordinary rates, with no state preference, plus the 1% surtax above $1M of taxable income.
