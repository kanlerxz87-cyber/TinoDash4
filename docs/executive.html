Executive summary and suggestions

Summary

  • Property A (Cupertino) is inherited through the trust. Property B (San Francisco, 94122) is already owned by the heir. The analysis treats this asymmetry explicitly: the choice is about Property A only.
  • Base case (transfer 2045, 10 years): selling Property A and investing ranks first, at about $3,911,160 in real wealth. Renting ranks lower at about $3,196,880 after taxes.
  • Living in Property A keeps the Prop 19 base year value, but the house produces no cash income. Its real total is about $2,217,150. Adding the imputed rent of about $490,940 (a non-cash value that is not spendable) gives about $2,708,090, still below the sell path.
  • The ranking changes in 5 of 10 stress sets. Phased entry ranks first in downside and crash cases; a rental path can rank first in the long lost-decade case.
  • Black Swan: a 1929-style fall at transfer would take a lump sum to about $823,940 in real wealth; the phased path would keep about $3,551,570, because the cash is parked during the fall. The 2008-style fall gives about $2,035,210 lump versus $3,815,990 phased.
  • Monte Carlo (5,000 runs, 20 years): the phased path beats lump sum in 55.3% of paths. Median lump $3,184,790; median phased $3,327,750.

These figures are model outputs under assumptions. Several inputs are unverified and are flagged (see Flagged for Review). They are not a recommendation to buy or sell.

Suggestions (for discussion with counsel and CPA)

  1. Obtain a written valuation of Property A at the date of death. The Prop 19 base year value and any sale price both depend on it.
  2. Confirm the Cupertino tax rate on the actual bill and the Santa Clara tax rate book (flag F-03).
  3. Decide, with the CPA, whether Property A will be occupied (Prop 19 homeowners' exemption within 1 year; claim within 3 years) or rented or sold. These are different legal paths.
  4. Get two insurance quotes for each property (flag F-08) and a rental-rate check for Property A (flag F-05).
  5. Keep any sale proceeds in short Treasuries until the phase-in plan is set. Re-check yields against the reinvestment-risk line before each tranche.
  6. Do not rely on the step-up cap in the reported Treasury proposal. It is not law (see Tax law).
