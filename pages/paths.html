Decision Paths Compared

All paths modeled at the same transfer dates and horizons, with taxes, debt, occupancy changes, capital costs, and liquidity. Dollar figures rounded to nearest $10.

Path 1 — Live in Cupertino (Primary Residence)

Live In Preferred if heir occupies

  • Prop 19: Primary-residence exclusion possible — must move in within 1 year
  • Property tax: Low — parent's base value carried forward (up to $1,044,586 cap)
  • Capital gains: Step-up in basis at death; exclusion on sale if later primary residence (2 of 5 years)
  • Housing cost: Replaces SF housing cost; SF home can remain rental
  • Insurance: Primary residence rate (~$1,500/yr est.)
  • Utilities: Water ~$100-$150/mo; solar credit ~$10-$20/mo
  • Downside: Cannot also claim SF as primary residence; loses rental option income on Cupertino

Reinvestment risk: None (no sale proceeds to stage)

Rent Out Cupertino Caution — Prop 19

  • Prop 19: NO exclusion for rental property — full reassessment to ~$3,100,000
  • Property tax: ~$36,000/yr (~1.162% × $3.1M) vs ~$7,200/yr now
  • Rental income: Market rent needed (not voucher benchmark) — obtain comps
  • Operating costs: Insurance, water, maintenance, property management
  • Capital gains: Deferred until sale; depreciation recapture if previously rented
  • Downside: High property tax may exceed rental income — rent path is a floor, not income source

Reinvestment risk: Staged rental income — reinvestment timing risk

Sell + Reinvest Consider for liquidity

  • Step-up basis: Basis = FMV at date of death (~$3,100,000 est.)
  • Capital gains: Only on post-death appreciation; CA taxes as ordinary income
  • Transfer tax & recording: Priced into sale (Santa Clara County)
  • Phased-investing: Park proceeds in 3M-2Y Treasuries 24-36 months, then phase into stocks
  • Black swan protection: Softens 1929/2008 crash entry risk
  • Downside: Loses Prop 13 low base permanently; loses appreciation on Cupertino

Reinvestment risk: Shown beside headline — dollar-cost averaging over 24-36 months reduces crash-entry risk

Path 2 — Mixed Paths (Not Only Pure Choices)

Live in Cupertino → Sell Later Mixed path

  • Claim Prop 19 exclusion (live in within 1 year)
  • After 2+ years as primary residence, sell with up to $250K/$500K gain exclusion
  • Reinvest proceeds per investment policy
  • SF home continues as rental

Rent Cupertino → Convert to Primary Later Mixed path

  • Rent initially; move in later (loses Prop 19 on rental period)
  • Partial reassessment during rental period; may re-qualify if converted
  • Test rental market before committing

Sell SF → Keep Cupertino Mixed path

  • Sell SF primary residence (up to $250K/$500K exclusion)
  • Use proceeds for Cupertino costs or reinvestment
  • Move into Cupertino to claim Prop 19
  • Simplifies to one primary residence

Keep Both → Rent Cupertino High carrying cost

  • SF as primary residence; Cupertino as rental
  • Cupertino fully reassessed (~$36,000/yr tax)
  • Rental income must cover tax + costs + vacancy
  • Likely negative cash flow — not recommended without comps

Phased-Investing Path (Black Swan Protection)

Strategy: If selling, park sale proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24-36 months after inheritance, then phase into stocks. This softens 1929/2008-style crash entry risk by avoiding lump-sum investment at market peaks.
