Phased investing test (1929 and 2008 entry risk)

Money is sold and held in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries (parking, taxed as ordinary income at an assumed 40%). It is then bought into stocks in equal steps over 12 months. The test uses the depth and timing of the two falls, not a daily replay. Each cell is the multiple of starting capital after tax (1.00x = no change).

Reading the table: phasing helps when a fall begins during the parking window. With no crash, a 24-month phase-in gives up about 9% of the 20-year value in this stylised test (about 5% in the base-case grid, where the stock path differs).

Decision points for this test

  • Parking is a hedge against entry timing, not a return enhancement.
  • A fall that starts after the parking window leaves the lump sum and the phase-in close; luck decides which one wins.
  • Parked money earns less than stocks in a calm market. The household accepts a lower median to buy protection.
