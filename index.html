Executive summary

  • Provisional lean (not a decision): in the base case, selling Property A in the transfer year and investing the proceeds ranks first. Phasing in over 24 months ranks second.
  • Base case: transfer in 2045, 20-year horizon, real 2026 dollars, no imputed rent credit. Sell and invest: $6,579,950. Phased over 24 months: $6,268,020. Rent then sell: $5,744,580. Live with no rent credit: $2,585,150.
  • Living in Property A ties with selling only if the household values avoided rent at about 97% of market rent.
  • Phasing is an insurance choice: in the base-case grid it gives up about 5% of the 20-year value if no crash comes (stylised no-crash test: about 9%), and it protects against an early 1929- or 2008-type fall.
  • Evidence gap: 4 input(s) are UNVERIFIED and left blank. Household inputs are held internally and shown only as placeholders.

What would change the answer

  • Living in Property A ties with selling only at an imputed rent credit of about 97% (base credit 0%).
  • Renting ties with selling if Property A's appreciation reaches about 3.4% a year (base 2.5%), or stock returns fall to about 4.9% a year.
  • Phasing in gives up about 5% of the base-case 20-year value if no crash comes, and reduces entry risk if a 1929- or 2008-type fall starts while the money is parked.
  • Prop 19: if the heir does not live in Property A, the property is reassessed to market on transfer. In 2045 terms (base assumptions), the property tax on the reassessed value is about $66,200 a year, against about $45,870 if the Prop 19 base applies.
  • Unknowns that could move the answer: the valuation at date of death, Property A's market rent, insurance quotes, estate size against the federal exclusion, and the heir's occupancy plan.
