EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A useful answer needs more than a home-price forecast.

Decision frame
01

Two different assets

Property A is the home expected to pass through a trust. Property B is already owned by the inheritor and has rental / occupancy economics. Never treat B as an inherited asset or assume its basis is reset.

02

Separate three tax systems

Federal estate tax applies to an estate under its rules; inherited asset income-tax basis is a separate question; California property-tax reassessment is separate again. A transfer is not automatically a recipient income-tax bill. Sources ↗

03

Compare the same date & horizon

Use the same inheritance/decision date, horizon, tax estimates, debt payoff, occupancy, capital plan and liquidity reserve for each path. Compare pure and mixed options, with reinvestment risk beside any staging result.

Compare paths on a common horizon

Enter rounded, non-identifying estimates only. All fields start blank. Property B is assumed retained in every pathway; its estimated horizon equity is shown in household totals, while its net cash flow changes with occupancy. Inputs are never stored or sent anywhere; refresh to clear.

Illustrative arithmetic · not tax advice
SHARED INPUTSUse advisor-estimated, after-tax values where requested.

The calculator is a sensitivity aid, not a tax, investment, valuation or legal model. Taxes are manually entered as adviser estimates; it does not calculate federal, state, transfer, depreciation-recapture, estate, property or Measure H taxes. It applies user-entered flat annual return / rent / expense-growth assumptions; it does not forecast parcel-specific reassessment, Proposition 19 adjustments, variable maintenance shocks, cash-flow timing, annual marginal tax brackets, transaction mechanics or a historical 1929 / 2008 sequence. The future sale tax and debt amounts are horizon estimates supplied by advisers. Property B is assumed retained in every path, with its entered horizon equity counted before any B sale cost or tax. Negative equity / sale-shortfall cases are rejected rather than modeled. Do not use it to transact.

CALIFORNIA · PROPERTY TAX

Proposition 19 / intergenerational transfer

BOE guidance describes the family-home exclusion conditions: the home must be the transferor's principal residence and the transferee's principal residence; its value limit is factored taxable value plus $1,000,000 (biennially adjusted); the transferee generally must occupy within one year and file for the homeowners' exemption within one year; the exclusion claim is generally due within three years or before a transfer to a third party. Eligibility is factual; this tool does not presume qualification or calculate the excess-value adjustment.

BOE: Proposition 19 ↗BOE information sheet (PDF) ↗
CALIFORNIA · ASSESSMENT

Proposition 13 and tax-rate areas

Assessed values are not market value; the annual inflation adjustment is capped at 2% under ordinary Prop 13 treatment, subject to decline-in-value rules. Parcel-specific voter debt / special charges require a private parcel-level rate and tax bill.

BOE: How property is assessed (PDF) ↗BOE: Santa Clara TRA map ↗Santa Clara County Assessor ↗
FEDERAL · ESTATE / GIFT

Estate tax, exclusion and portability

IRS 2026 Form 706 instructions state a $15,000,000 basic exclusion for decedents dying in 2026, with inflation adjustments and prior taxable gifts relevant. This is a 2026 figure, not a 2040–2050 forecast. The return is generally due nine months after death; consult the current instructions.

IRS: 2026 Form 706 instructions ↗IRS: Form 706 ↗
FEDERAL · INCOME-TAX BASIS

Inherited basis, depreciation and property

IRS Publication 551 describes inherited-property basis generally by reference to fair market value at death (subject to exceptions and elections). Rental conversion depreciation and later gain require a tax professional's asset-level analysis.

IRS Publication 551 ↗IRS Publication 527: residential rental property ↗
FEDERAL · PRINCIPAL RESIDENCE

Home-sale exclusion

Current IRS Publication 523 describes up to $250,000 ($500,000 for qualifying joint filers) of gain exclusion if ownership, use, and other requirements are met. Living in a home is not by itself enough; rental use, depreciation and timing matter.

IRS Publication 523 ↗
CALIFORNIA · INCOME TAX

One-percent tax and capital gain treatment

The 2025 Form 540 instructions compute the 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax on the amount of an individual filer’s California taxable income above $1,000,000. FTB states California has no lower capital-gains rate; gains are taxed as ordinary income. These current rules may change before inheritance; exact filing status, taxable income and basis require CPA review. This model uses no assumed rate.

FTB: 2025 Form 540 instructions, line 62 ↗FTB: capital gains and losses ↗
FEDERAL · LIKE-KIND EXCHANGE

1031 exchange decision

IRC section 1031 can defer gain on qualifying real property held for business / investment, not a personal residence. Eligibility, deadlines, qualified intermediary rules and subsequent personal-use treatment need specialist advice before sale.

IRS Publication 544 ↗IRS: sale of your home ↗
CALIFORNIA + FEDERAL · INHERITED ASSETS

Inheritance vs. income tax

California Revenue and Taxation Code §13301 bars state/local gift, inheritance, succession, legacy and estate taxes. IRS guidance says inherited property generally is not included in a beneficiary's income, but later interest, dividends, rents, trust income or retirement distributions may be taxable. Property tax reassessment, local parcel charges and transaction taxes are separate; confirm each asset and filing with advisers.

California Revenue and Taxation Code §13301 ↗IRS Publication 17 ↗IRS Publication 559: estates and beneficiaries ↗

QUICK REFERENCE

Glossary

Plain language
Estate tax

Tax imposed on a decedent's taxable estate under applicable law. It is not the same as a beneficiary's income tax.

Income-tax basis

Tax cost used to compute gain or loss. Inherited property is generally assigned a date-of-death fair-market-value basis, subject to exceptions and elections; document the appraisal.

Assessed value / FBYV

Property-tax value. Under ordinary Prop 13 rules, base-year value is factored annually up to the lesser of inflation or 2%, subject to change-in-ownership, new construction and decline-in-value rules.

Proposition 19 exclusion

A conditional California property-tax reassessment exclusion for qualifying intergenerational transfers of a family home / farm. It is not an income-tax basis adjustment.

Imputed rent

An estimate of the housing service the household receives by living in a property instead of renting an equivalent home. It is an economic comparison input, not cash income or an automatic tax deduction.

Depreciation recapture

Tax treatment on sale related to depreciation allowed or allowable for rental/business use. It can make some gain taxable even when other home-sale exclusion rules apply.

DSUE / portability

A deceased spouse's unused federal exclusion potentially transferred to the surviving spouse when an executor makes a valid portability election, generally on a timely Form 706.

1031 exchange

A tax deferral mechanism for qualifying investment/business real property exchanges. It is not a simple sale-and-reinvest rule and requires advance structuring.

Tax-rate area (TRA)

A geographic area with a unique combination of taxing districts. The parcel's actual rate must be checked in official records; a county average is not its rate.

Reinvestment risk

The risk that maturing fixed-income proceeds must be reinvested at lower yields. Staging also risks missing stock gains before entry.
