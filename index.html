Inherited-home decision dashboard

Property A is a single-family house in Santa Clara County that the heir expects to inherit through a trust, around 2040 to 2050. Property B is a three-unit building in San Francisco County that the heir already owns. This site compares eight paths for Property A at identical transfer years and horizons, with taxes, debt, occupancy, capital costs, liquidity and reinvestment risk.

What would change the answer

  1. A written commitment by the heir to live in A within 12 months. Until it exists, Rule 3 removes the living paths. Even with it, living matches a lump-sum sale only if the imputed rent yield is about 7.6% of value a year (base assumption 3.75%) or if home values grow about 7.3% a year (base 2.3%).
  2. Three verified rental comparables for A. Until they exist, Rule 4 keeps the rent paths as a floor only. A rent path matches a lump-sum sale only at a gross yield of above 20%, outside the range tested.
  3. Tax-rate area and bills for A. The owner's billed rate is 1.34% of assessed value; the reference rate area used here is 1.04% (FY 2024-25). The parcel's own rate area is unknown. The model uses the billed rate. When the reference rate is used instead, the pick does not change (see What-if).
  4. Equity returns. If equities return 5% a year instead of the historical 10.02%, the pick does not change in any of the four cases (see What-if).
  5. The Proposition 19 allowance. The statutory amount of $1,044,586 applies to transfers up to 15 February 2027. The next adjustment is due 16 February 2027 (BOE LTA 2025/009). A change would re-open the living case.
  6. Other estate assets above the federal exclusion. Known real estate is about $4,900,000 (owner estimates). The federal basic exclusion is $15,000,000 for 2026, so estate tax is not modelled unless the total estate is verified above that amount (Rule 7).

Pick for the 2050 transfer, 20-year horizon

Pick under the rules written on 25 November 2026, before any output existed: Sell, lump sum. Median real financial position at the horizon: $20,199,660 (10th percentile $6,720,000, 90th percentile $56,435,280). The phased path (park 24 months, then phase in) has a median of $16,211,010, which is 19.7% below the pick, outside the 5% band in Rule 5. Under a repeat of the 1929-type crash, the pick gives $4,626,110 and the 24-month phased path gives $9,683,850; see phased investing.

This is a comparison of stated assumptions. It is not a forecast and not advice.
