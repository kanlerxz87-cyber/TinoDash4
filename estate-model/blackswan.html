Black swan tests

Two stylised crashes start 12 months after transfer. The drop sizes come from the Federal Reserve History essay on the 1929 crash and the 2008 Great Recession; the timing and recovery lengths are illustrative assumptions [F20].

  • 1929-type: equities fall about 89% from peak to trough over 34 months, then return to the pre-crash peak about 268 months later (nominal peak regained November 1954, per the Fed History essay). Home prices fall 30% over 12 months [F21].
  • 2008-type: equities fall about 57% over 17 months (October 2007 to March 2009, per the Fed History essay), back to peak in 48 months after trough (assumed).

Sources: Federal Reserve History: 1929. Peak-relative recovery means the lost compounding does not come back.
