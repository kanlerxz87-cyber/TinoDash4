Estate tax, income-tax basis and property tax: three separate items

Each item below has its own rule and its own source. They are not combined into one number.

2. Income-tax basis

  • Inherited property generally takes basis at fair market value on the date of death. Pub. 551 gives examples; the statute text (IRC §1014) is not yet read [F06]. Source: IRS Publication 551, Basis of Assets.
  • Written valuation at the date of death is needed before any sale. Sale gain is measured from that basis.
  • Converting personal use to rental changes the depreciation basis (lesser of FMV or adjusted basis at the change). Source: IRS Publication 551, Basis of Assets. CPA to confirm recapture.
  • Like-kind exchange (§1031): the CPA should advise before any sale; the statute text is not yet read [F06].
  • Capital-gain rates applied in the model: 20% federal top (flagged [F13]), 3.8% NIIT (flagged [F13]), and CA 12.30% plus 1% surcharge over $1,000,000 (FTB 2025 Form 540 booklet; California Revenue & Taxation Code §17043).

3. Property tax and Prop 19

  • Parent-child exclusion (RTC §69.6, effective 4/1/2021) applies only to the principal residence. Source: BOE.
  • Taxable value for an occupied home: the lesser of fair market value and factored base-year value plus the indexed amount ($1,044,586 for 2/16/2025 to 2/15/2027). Source: BOE Letter to Assessors 2025/009. Next adjustment due 2/16/2027.
  • Child occupies the home within 1 year and claims the homeowners' exemption; claim within 3 years or before a third-party transfer. Source: BOE Letter to Assessors 2026/026 (chunks 0 to 2). [F07]
  • Property not occupied (path B, or the rented share of E) is reassessed at full value. The model uses full value as the fallback when the base-year value is blank [F08].
  • The Prop 13 inflation factor for 2026-27 is 1.02; growth in taxable value is capped at the lesser of 2% or CPI. Source: BOE Letter to Assessors 2026/002.
