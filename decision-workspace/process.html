The inheritance process, step by step

The route below is the trust route, which is what the household expects: the house is held in a trust and passes outside probate. The dates are the ones that actually bind - a missed one-year filing is not recoverable by argument.

#StageWhat to doSource
1Before the death - the trust routeNothing in this list requires probate if the house is titled in the trust. Confirm with the estate attorney that Property A is in the trust NOW, that the trust is revocable and the settlor is the occupant, and who the successor trustees are. If the house is not in the trust, the timeline below is optimistic by 12-18 months.no source located
2At the death - documents to obtainCertified death certificates (several), the trust instrument and any amendments, the trailer/assignment of personal property, the property's most recent tax bill and assessment record, the mortgage statement (if any), the insurance policy, and the utility accounts. Everything else follows from these.IRS-559 official
3Valuation - the single most load-bearing documentCommission a written, dated appraisal of Property A at the date of death (and of the contents if material). It sets income-tax basis, the Form 706 number if one is filed, and the Prop 19 excess calculation. Get it from a licensed appraiser, not an estate agent's market analysis, and keep the unrounded copy offline.BOE-P19 official
4Estate tax decisionAdd the gross estate plus post-1976 taxable gifts. If it exceeds the threshold for the year of death ($15,000,000 for 2026), Form 706 is due nine months after death, on extension via Form 4768. Even when no tax is due, file 706 if portability of a prior spouse's unused exclusion is wanted.IRS-ESTATE official
5Distribution and the change in ownershipThe trustee's distribution to the beneficiary is the property-tax event. File the change in ownership statement (BOE-502-D) within 150 days. Expect supplemental bills afterwards - they are for the part-year, not a mistake.SCC-RATEBOOK official
6Property tax: two claims, two deadlinesIf the heir will live there: (a) file for the homeowners' exemption within ONE year, and (b) file the BOE-19-P parent-child claim within THREE years (or before any transfer to a third party). Miss (a) and (b) is void. If the heir will rent it, do not file them - the exclusion is unavailable and the value reassesses in full.BOE-P19 official
7Insurance and utilities from the date of distributionNotify the insurer (vacancy changes the policy), re-title the utility accounts, and if the solar system is leased/PPA find out whether the contract is assignable before listing.no source located
8Choose a path against the written criteriaNot against the projection. The criteria on /framework.html were agreed before the numbers; score each path against them, then look at the money. Record the choice and the date in the decision log.no source located
9If selling: price, then stage, then invest per the IPSDecide the 1031 question in writing BEFORE listing. Then follow the investment policy - the Treasury parking lane and the phase-in calendar - which was agreed while there was nothing to argue about.IRS-544 official
10After: re-run once a year/recheck.html and /watch.html. The transfer may be 15+ years away; the rules in this workspace will not all still be true in 2045, and some will have changed by 2028.no source located

Everything relative ('within 1 year of transfer') is measured from the distribution, not from the death, and the difference between those two dates can be the difference between having Prop 19 and not having it. Q-08 asks which.

What the trust route does and does not do

It avoids
  • Probate, and the 12-18 months and publication requirements that come with it
  • A public record of the transfer in most cases
  • Contest exposure between beneficiaries, if the instrument is clean
It does not avoid
  • A change in ownership for property-tax purposes when the trustee distributes to the beneficiary
  • The date-of-death valuation requirement - an appraisal is still needed
  • Form 706 if the estate exceeds the federal threshold
  • Medi-Cal estate claims, which attach to the property regardless of the trust
  • Income tax on the gain if the heir later sells above the stepped-up basis
