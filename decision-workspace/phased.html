The phased-investing lane

The household's proposal, tested rather than described: after the sale, hold the proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24-36 months, then move into equities in monthly instalments, so a crash immediately after the inheritance cannot take the whole stake. Below is what that does in the ten worst entry years of the last century, and what it costs in the rest of them.

Current environment

The Instruments in the lane pay what the curve says, not what history averaged: on the Treasury published curve for 2026 the 3-month stood at 3.65% and the 2-year at 3.47%, with the 10-year at 4.19%. A parking lane therefore costs much less to hold in 2026 than it did in 2012, and the whole comparison below shifts when the short rate moves. UST-DAILY official

The verdict, with the bill attached

ClaimSupported?EvidenceSource
Staging softens a crash immediately after the saleyes, decisivelyEntering 1929, a 24-month park plus 24-month phase ends 30 years later +168% ahead of lump-sum and cuts the deepest drawdown by 26 points. Entering 2007 it is +19% with the same drawdown reduction. It works because the two things being mixed - bills and equities - are negatively correlated in exactly those years.DAMO academic UST-DAILY official
Staging improves the average outcomenoOver the full record, moving money in later means missing the up days as often as the down ones. In the model's own Monte Carlo the staged paths give up about 10% of terminal wealth on average.DAMO academic UST-DAILY official
Longer is betterno36 months of parking is never better than 24 on the crash years and always costs more elsewhere. If the lane is wanted, 24 months is the defensible number.DAMO academic UST-DAILY official
Cash is a safe place to waitonly when it paysIn 2008-09 bills paid 1.40% then 0.15%; in 2021-22 they paid 0.04% and 2.09% while the house and the market both stalled. The lane is only 'safe' to the extent the short rate covers inflation, which is a fact about the year you enter, not a property of T-bills.DAMO academic UST-DAILY official
Reinvestment risk is the real risk of stagingyesThe 2020 and 2021 entries show the mirror image: parking through a recovery is the one way to lose to lump-sum investing on the drawdown metric as well as the return metric, because you buy back in at the high. Any pitch for staging has to carry this sentence.DAMO academic UST-DAILY official

Investment policy for the proceeds, in writing before the money exists

  1. Two thirds of net proceeds into a globally diversified, low-cost equity index; one third into short-duration Treasuries. Rebalance when either side moves 5 points off target.
  2. Whatever the household needs within five years - capital works, taxes, a possible buyout of a co-beneficiary - is held in Treasuries maturing inside that window and is not part of the equity allocation at all.
  3. A liquidity floor of 24 months of all carrying costs for both buildings plus a capital-events reserve, held in cash and maturing bills, agreed before any illiquid choice is made.
  4. The parking lane, if chosen, is 24 months maximum, with the equity tranche moved in on a fixed calendar (monthly), not on a market condition. No 'waiting for a better entry' clause - that clause is how a policy becomes a wager.
  5. No leverage, no single-stock positions, no second property purchase without re-running this model, and no decision at all inside the first 90 days after the death.
  6. The policy is signed and dated, and any change to it requires a written reason. Its whole value is that it was written when nothing was happening.

This is a starting draft for the household and its advisers, not advice, and it is deliberately short: a policy nobody can read is a policy nobody follows.
