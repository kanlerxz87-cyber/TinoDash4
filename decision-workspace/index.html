Property A & B - inherited house decision workspace

Two buildings, one decision: live in the inherited house, rent it, sell it, or some order of those. Everything below is generated by a model whose inputs are sourced and whose gaps are visible. As of 2026-11-25.

What would change the answer

This workspace ranks twelve ways of handling Property A. The ranking is not fragile with respect to house prices or the stock market, and it is entirely fragile with respect to the six things below. Each is stated with the number it moves.

Whether the heir actually lives there

Prop 19 is worth $20,300 a year in year one and about $860,310 over thirty years. Occupying within a year and filing the homeowners' exemption is the only thing that buys it. Rent the house instead and the exclusion is void.

BOE-P19 official
What the rent really is

Every rent-path number currently rests on the owner's estimate of $7,500-$9,500 a month, not on closed comparables. Four signed leases of comparable single-family houses in the same submarket could move the rent paths above or below the live paths. See Comparables.

The parcel's actual tax rate area

The household's own bills imply about 1.33% of assessed value, above the county's published 1.00000% maximum levy plus the bonds it could be matched to. If the true composite is higher, every keep path gets worse and the sell paths get better. This is the single most valuable missing number on the site.

SCC-RATEBOOK official
Whether a sale happens in the year of the death

A sale at transfer has effectively no gain because the basis is the date-of-death value. Waiting ten years and renting turns that into $1,050,680 of tax on a $7,305,350 sale. The step-up is generous and it is time-limited in practice.

IRS-559 official
Depreciation, and the recapture it creates

Renting A for ten years shelters about $1,482,510 of rent, and then recaptures it at up to 25% plus 3.8% on the way out. That trade is worth re-pricing every time the rent estimate changes.

IRS-946 official
The household's own constraints, which the model cannot see

A co-beneficiary who will not sign, a Medi-Cal estate claim, an unpermitted third unit at Property B, or a cash need inside five years each override a $29,405,410 spread. None of them are in the model, and three of them are still unknown.

Executive summary

1. The money answer and the life answer are different answers. On real terminal wealth alone, selling Property A in the year of the death (P-05) is worth about $33,992,380 in 2026 dollars at a thirty-year horizon, against about $4,620,130 for living in it and leaving Property B's third unit empty. That gap is not a forecast of the housing market. It is the price of holding an asset that lets out for 1.9% of its value gross, and nothing else - while the same money, invested, earns the market's return without a tax bill attached.

2. Living there is cheap in the ways that matter and expensive in one. The one expensive thing is the assessment: $52,210 a year under Prop 19 versus $72,510 if the house is rented. Everything else about occupying the house - insurance, water, the solar credit, even upkeep - is smaller than that single line, and the Prop 19 saving compounds at 2% a year for as long as it is held.

3. The rent paths are not evidence-based yet. Two of the twelve paths rank partly on a rent figure the household supplied. Until comparables arrive, treat those rankings as provisional, and note that a voucher-anchored estimate was deliberately not used.

4. Staging the proceeds costs return and buys something real. Parking the sale proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24 months and phasing into equities costs about 10.5% of terminal wealth in the middle of history and improves the outcome entering 1929, 1973 and 2000. The page on phased investing shows both sides, because a headline about staging without a reinvestment-risk number beside it is marketing.

5. Property B is not a second decision, it is the reason the first one is affordable. The household already owns a three-unit building it can move into. That is what makes 'live in A now, decide later' genuinely reversible, and it is also where the new San Francisco parcel tax and any owner-move-in eviction exposure land.

6. Nothing here is a reason to hurry, and two things are reasons to move now. The transfer is years away. But the appraisal, the tax-rate-area lookup and the written agreement about what each person wants are cheap, independent of the future, and each one changes what the model can say.

Do these five things before arguing about any projection

  1. A date-of-death appraisal is needed for basis, for the Form 706 (if filed) and for the Prop 19 excess calculation. Same document, three uses, and it is cheapest to obtain once. S-03 effort low / impact high
  2. Only 'sell' is irreversible. Live-in for 12-24 months, keep Prop 19 as a live option by occupying and filing for the homeowners' exemption, and decide the sale later with better information. The model prices that option: it is path P-04. S-01 effort low / impact high
  3. Asset allocation, the Treasury parking lane, rebalancing bands, permitted leverage, and the redeployment calendar - agreed and dated now. See /ips.html. S-04 effort medium / impact high
  4. Two houses plus a fixed income is a fragile balance sheet. Set the floor (24 months of all carrying costs plus a capital-events reserve) in the IPS and make the paths satisfy it before comparing returns. S-10 effort low / impact high
  5. Each rule row carries a status: enacted (with effective date) or proposed (with sponsor and stage). The Prop H row is the live example: approved by voters on 2026-11-03 but not certified until 2026-12-03 and not billed until FY 2027-28. S-07 effort low / impact medium

How to read this site

Blank means no source

A cell showing not verified is a value that could not be read from an official document in this pass. It is never filled with an estimate.

Every table row is linked

The Source column carries the document the row came from, with its verification status. Official, academic, owner-supplied and unverified are different words and they are used differently here.

Ranges, not points

Assumptions are stored as low/mid/high and the model is run across the grid, so a headline that only survives at one point on a dial is reported as not robust.

Three tax systems, kept apart

Estate tax at death, income tax on the gain, and property tax on the assessment are separate tables with separate deadlines. Conflating them is the classic error in this decision.

Open questions are the product

20 questions are unresolved. Each says who can answer it and how. The model is a way of noticing them; the answers are the work.

Suggestions first, then code

Twelve method proposals are on a board for the household to accept or reject before more implementation follows.
