How the model works

The model is a small, boring Python program. This page is its honest documentation: what it computes, the conventions it uses, the things it refuses to guess, and how to make it say something new.

Conventions, stated so they can be attacked

  • Paths are ranked on terminal household wealth assuming everything is sold at market and the tax on the unrealised gain is paid. It is a liquidation measure: it deliberately ignores that a house you live in is also something you consume, which is what the imputed-rent column is for.
  • Cash flow that a path generates or consumes runs through a household account earning the bill rate - an opportunity-cost convention. Charging the live paths a consumer-loan spread for a shortfall they would fund from savings would have biased the answer toward selling.
  • Rental losses are treated as suspended rather than deductible against salary, and depreciation shelters the rent until it does not. That is conservative for the rent paths and flagged as unconfirmed.
  • Inflation enters the tax base only through the Prop 13 cap: whenever CPI runs above 2% the assessed value grows slower than prices, so a keep path looks better in real terms in a high-inflation world. That is the rule, not a modelling artefact - and it is the quietest strong argument for holding.
  • Every dollar figure leaves the build through one rounding step and one text filter, so the published site cannot be traced back to a public record. Exact values exist only in the household's offline folder.
  • Values with no official source are blank. The blank is the finding.
  • Nothing is modelled on proposed legislation, and nothing is modelled on a figure from a search result that was not then read in the underlying document.

What it cannot do

  • It cannot tell you the rent, because nobody has gathered the comparables yet. Two of the twelve paths are therefore provisional in a way the others are not.
  • It cannot tell you the parcel's actual tax rate. Both the county's published composites and the household's own bills imply rates that do not reconcile, and the difference is the second largest line in the model.
  • It cannot see the family. A co-owner, a wish, a dispute, or a Medi-Cal claim is outside every table here and inside the outcome.
  • It cannot forecast 2045. The transfer year is a placeholder inside a ten-year window (2040-2050) and there is no dial for it here, because a dial would imply the date was a matter of taste rather than of fact. Narrowing that window is the estate attorney's job, not the model's.
  • Its tails are as wide as the record is long: a 98-year sample of annual returns cannot tell you the probability of a decade that has not happened in it.

Reproducing it

cd model
python3 build.py     # model -> decision-workspace/data/*.json  (rounded + scrubbed)
python3 site.py      # data -> ../ *.html and assets/data.js
python3 engine.py    # prints the run summary, no files written

The Monte Carlo is seeded (seed 20261125, 1200 simulations, three-year blocks), so the same inputs always produce the same tables. The environment needs nothing but the standard library - no numpy, no pandas - which is why the simulation is small enough to run at build time instead of in the browser.
