Black swans and regime switching

Fifty years of growth and inflation are never smooth. This page is the answer to 'what if the next thirty years do not look like the last thirty, or look like the worst of them'. Everything here is computed from the annual record, not quoted from memory, and the record itself is academic rather than official - see F-9.

Ten-year rolling returns are the honest version of 'average return': the middle of the record is 11.0% a year, the 5th percentile of overlapping ten-year windows is -0.6%, and 5.7% of all ten-year windows in the whole record lost money in real terms. Anyone quoting 10% as a plan input is quoting the mean of a distribution whose bottom decile is negative.

The regime that breaks the rent path is not a crash, it is a yield. At the modelled transfer the gross rent on Property A is about 1.9% of its value, and net operating income at the owner's own rent estimate is -$16,400 - negative, before any income tax, because the reassessed tax bill alone ($72,510) is larger than the rent left after upkeep. To match what the same money earns in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries after tax (2.4%), the rent would have to be $20,610 a month, which is 142% above the estimate used so far. That is why no amount of optimism about stock returns rescues the rent paths, and why the comparables work on this page matters more than any assumption about the market.
