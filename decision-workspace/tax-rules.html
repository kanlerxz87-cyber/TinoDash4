Tax rules that apply, verified where possible

Three tax systems apply to this decision. They have different authorities, different deadlines and different rules, and the most common way an inherited-house plan goes wrong is by treating one of them as if it answered another. They are kept apart below, and every rule carries its status: enacted and read, enacted but not read, passed and awaiting certification, proposed, or unverified.

Estate / transfer tax

Levied on the transfer at death. Federal only in our case. Decides whether a Form 706 is due, and whether the estate is large enough to need planning at all. It does not change the property tax bill.

Income tax

Levied on the gain when something is sold, and on rent while it is rented. The step-up at death lives here - it resets basis to date-of-death value. It has no effect on the assessment.

Property tax

Levied every year on the assessed value by the county. Proposition 13 and Proposition 19 live here. A step-up in basis does nothing for it, and it does not care about your gain.

San Francisco's transfer tax is a cliff, not a marginal schedule: at $1M-$5M the tier of $3.75 per $500 applies to the entire consideration, so a small price move across the $5,000,000 line changes the bill by thousands. Property B at $1.8M sits two tiers below it; a future sale of the building after further appreciation would not.
