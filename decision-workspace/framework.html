Decision rules agreed in advance

These criteria were written before the projections were argued about, and they exist so that the projection cannot be reverse-engineered into a justification. If a number moves and the thresholds still hold, the answer has not changed. If the number moves and the answer does, the honest conclusion is that the decision was never inside the projection.

Why this page exists

Fix the thresholds that make each choice correct, in writing, before arguing about any projection. If a projection moves and the thresholds still hold, the answer has not changed.

Choose 'live in Property A' when

  1. The household intends Property A to be their principal residence for at least 5 years (that is what makes Prop 19 and later section 121 real, and what justifies the reassessment).
  2. Gross rental yield on A is BELOW the after-tax yield available on Treasury bills - i.e. the rental market is not paying them enough to give up the liquidity.
  3. Written insurance quotes for the owner-occupied house are below the modelled figure, and the 5-year capital plan needs under $150,000.
  4. No co-beneficiary needs cash, and no creditor or Medi-Cal claim can attach to the house.
  5. They can fund the annual shortfall between A's carrying cost and Property B's net operating income without borrowing.

Choose 'keep it as a rental' when

  1. Four closed leases for comparable Santa Clara County single-family houses show net yield (after tax, insurance, upkeep, vacancy and management) above the after-tax yield on Treasuries by at least 0.5 percentage points.
  2. The household accepts that a non-owner-occupied inherited house gets NO Prop 19 exclusion: the model's rent paths therefore carry a fully reassessed tax bill from year one.
  3. Depreciation is wanted, and the household has read the recapture line in this workspace and accepts the deferred-tax trade.
  4. The rental losses will be suspended under the passive rules - i.e. there is no salary offset.

Choose 'sell' when

  1. Net after-tax proceeds would produce more income in a diversified portfolio than the house produces net, at the household's own risk tolerance, for the whole planning horizon.
  2. The household needs liquidity, wants to consolidate debt, or a co-owner requires cash.
  3. Expected carrying costs plus the reassessment penalty exceed the rent they can actually prove.
  4. They will not live in it - in which case Prop 19 is unavailable and the tax argument for holding collapses.

Choose a mixed path when

  1. Occupancy is uncertain, or the housing/rent evidence is still thin: live in A, claim the exemption and the exclusion, and defer the sale decision by 24-36 months. This keeps every door open, costs only the option's own carrying cost, and it is the only path that preserves both Prop 19 and section 121.

Hard stops - not trade-offs

  • Any Medi-Cal estate claim, tax lien or judgment against the property.
  • An unpermitted unit at Property B that the insurer or a buyer will not accept.
  • A co-owner who will not sign (partition risk).
  • A liquidity shortfall that forces a sale in whatever year it happens.
Tie-break, agreed in advance: If two paths are within 10% of each other on real terminal wealth, choose the more reversible one. Reversibility is worth more than a modelling margin.

How the ranking is computed

score = 100 × ( 0.50 × normalised median real terminal wealth
          + 0.25 × normalised 10th percentile
          + 0.15 × (1 - relative spread of outcomes)
          + 0.10 × liquidity / no-forced-sale flag )

How to add to it

One line per decision, in model/registers.py under DECISION_LOG: date, what was decided, why, status, who owns it. A decision that reverses an earlier one keeps both lines, so the record shows the reversal instead of quietly replacing it. The alternative - a spreadsheet in which only the current answer survives - is how households argue with themselves three times about the same house.

Built as a decision workspace, not advice. Every figure on this site is rounded to the nearest $10 so it cannot be matched to a public record; exact values are only in the household's offline folder.
